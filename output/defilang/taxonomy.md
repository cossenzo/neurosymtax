# Taxonomy: Decentralized finance (DeFi)

Meta-characteristic: The taxonomy views DeFi through the lens of how its community discourse, in the form of jargon, slang and warning expressions, signals and describes risks and harms to consumers, linking these sociolinguistic patterns to technical concepts and risk categories to support consumer protection.

Status: completed. 

## Consumer risk

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Harm mechanism | Market manipulation harm; Fraud, abandonment or illicit fund flows; Security or custody failure; Excessive speculative exposure; Non-risk community signal; Structural or systemic market failure |
| Affected party | Late or new buyers; Existing holders of an asset; Users of a platform or protocol; Multiple participants or the whole community; No consumer group affected; Individual traders exposed through own positions; Victims of fraud or unauthorised resource use |

## Community discourse

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Discourse function | Risk warning; Accusation of a harmful actor; Neutral description of a phenomenon; Hype or enthusiasm; Community bonding or greeting |
| Lexical origin | Gaming-origin term; Technical or hacker term; Crypto-native term; General or financial-market term |
| Referent type | Market actor or participant group; Attack or fraud technique; Systemic event or chain reaction; Market condition or price pattern; Coin or asset status; Technical mechanism or product design; Participant mindset or in-group phrase |
| Posting tactic | Templated or coordinated posting; Ticker piggybacking on a popular asset; Dismissal of critics; No distinctive discourse tactic |

## Definitions

**Harm mechanism** (Consumer risk). The kind of consumer harm that the community term signals or describes. *Application rule:* Identify the harm the term most directly signals to consumers and choose the matching characteristic. Use Non-risk community signal when the term signals no harm.

- *Market manipulation harm*: Harm caused by insiders, large holders or transaction-ordering exploits such as front-running that distort prices or trade execution to the detriment of later or other buyers.
- *Fraud, abandonment or illicit fund flows*: Harm caused by developers or projects that withdraw funds, disappear or act fraudulently, or by exposure to illicit fund flows such as laundering.
- *Security or custody failure*: Harm caused by software vulnerabilities, exploits, attacks or loss of control over assets held with a custodian.
- *Excessive speculative exposure*: Harm arising from highly speculative, impulsive or leveraged positions and unrealistic return expectations.
- *Non-risk community signal*: Term that does not signal a consumer harm, such as community bonding, greeting, enthusiasm, status or a neutral market label.
- *Structural or systemic market failure*: Harm arising from design trade-offs, liquidity shortfalls, collateral mechanics, credit exposure or market-wide downturns rather than from deliberate actors or code exploits.

**Affected party** (Consumer risk). The group of participants that bears the harm signalled by the community term. *Application rule:* Choose the party most directly exposed to the harm the term signals, applying the characteristics in the order listed; the first matching characteristic applies: late or new buyers, existing holders of an asset, users of a platform or protocol, individual traders exposed through their own positions, victims of fraud or unauthorised resource use, multiple participants or the whole community, otherwise no consumer group affected.

- *Late or new buyers*: The harm falls on participants who buy after the price has already risen or after early insiders have sold.
- *Existing holders of an asset*: The harm falls on participants who already hold the asset and keep it through a decline.
- *Users of a platform or protocol*: The harm falls on users whose funds or data depend on a platform, protocol or custodian.
- *Multiple participants or the whole community*: The harm or benefit is described as affecting several participants or the community as a whole.
- *No consumer group affected*: No identifiable consumer group is stated as affected, for example a gaming role, a status label, a neutral market term or a stated personal trading behaviour.
- *Individual traders exposed through own positions*: The harm falls on an individual participant who commits a large share of their own funds to a concentrated or leveraged position and can lose it, for example through a forced liquidation.
- *Victims of fraud or unauthorised resource use*: The harm falls on participants who are deceived into a transaction or whose devices or resources are used without consent, such as through scams or cryptojacking.

**Discourse function** (Community discourse). The communicative act that the community term performs in forums and social media. *Application rule:* Identify the primary communicative act of the term in its usage: accusation when it names an actor, otherwise warning, neutral description, hype or community bonding.

- *Risk warning*: The term warns readers about a risk, a harm or a dangerous situation.
- *Accusation of a harmful actor*: The term accuses or names others as responsible for manipulation, fraud, dumping or reckless trading.
- *Neutral description of a phenomenon*: The term describes a market state, event or practice without warning or accusing anyone.
- *Hype or enthusiasm*: The term expresses optimism, excitement or rally support for an asset or project.
- *Community bonding or greeting*: The term builds in-group membership, greets the community or signals shared status.

**Lexical origin** (Community discourse). The domain from which the community term originates or in which its meaning is grounded. *Application rule:* Use the origin stated in the passages. Apply the characteristics in the order listed; the first one that matches the stated origin applies.

- *Gaming-origin term*: The term originates from online games and keeps a game-specific meaning, such as a game role or tactic.
- *Technical or hacker term*: The term originates from computing, software security or hacker culture.
- *Crypto-native term*: The term originates within the crypto community, for example from a forum post or a crypto event.
- *General or financial-market term*: The term is a general slang or market expression not tied to a gaming, technical or crypto-native origin.

**Referent type** (Community discourse). The kind of thing the community term refers to. *Application rule:* Identify what the term refers to and apply the characteristics in the order listed; the first one that matches applies: a named actor or participant group first, then an attack or fraud technique, then a discrete event or self-reinforcing chain reaction with system-wide consequences, then a market-wide condition or price pattern, then a coin or asset status, then a participant mindset or in-group phrase, otherwise a technical mechanism or product design.

- *Market actor or participant group*: The term names a person, a group identity or a subculture of participants.
- *Attack or fraud technique*: The term names a method used to exploit a system, deceive participants or defraud them.
- *Systemic event or chain reaction*: The term names a discrete event, failure or self-reinforcing chain reaction with system-wide consequences.
- *Market condition or price pattern*: The term names a market-wide state, a cycle phase or a price pattern.
- *Coin or asset status*: The term names a class of coins or the status of an asset, such as a dead or non-Bitcoin coin.
- *Technical mechanism or product design*: The term names a blockchain mechanism, token design, product or security practice.
- *Participant mindset or in-group phrase*: The term names a psychological stance, an investment behaviour or an in-group greeting or phrase of participants, such as fear of missing out, holding through a downturn or heavy commitment.

**Posting tactic** (Community discourse). The distinctive posting or promotion technique that the community term relies on in forums and social media. *Application rule:* Identify whether the term or its post relies on a distinctive technique: templated or coordinated posting first, then ticker piggybacking on a popular asset, then derogatory dismissal of critics; otherwise no distinctive tactic.

- *Templated or coordinated posting*: The term appears in posts that reuse a set of seemingly identical textual templates.
- *Ticker piggybacking on a popular asset*: The term mentions a popular asset's ticker to draw trader attention to a different, low-volume asset.
- *Dismissal of critics*: The term mocks or dismisses those who doubt or criticise the asset class.
- *No distinctive discourse tactic*: A stated absence of templated posting, ticker piggybacking or dismissal of critics.
