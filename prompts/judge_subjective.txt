How to judge:
- A condition fails only for a relevant or serious problem that significantly compromises it with respect to the purpose and the target users. Imperfections, matters of taste, wording preferences and possible improvements do not make a condition fail; a taxonomy need not be ideal, only adequate. When in doubt, the condition is met.
- Do not raise again an issue that the operations above resolved, and do not reverse a change made at your own suggestion (for example, do not ask to remove what you asked to add) unless it causes a serious problem; if so, say so explicitly.
- Do not ask for elements for which no classified object exists (they would need new evidence), and do not penalise a characteristic merely for having few objects when it is needed for the purpose (for example a negation).
- Give at most three suggestions per failed condition, only those needed for it to pass, each one concrete and executable on the existing taxonomy (rename, merge, split, delete, change a definition).
